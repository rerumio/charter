# Post types

## TL;DR

A type says what sort of text a post is and what the platform requires of it: whom it is written
for, what its claims rest on, and what it must carry beyond the body. Its authors choose the type
before publishing, and nothing changes it afterwards. It decides what a post owes its reader, and
nothing else.

## Definition

A type says what sort of text a post is: whom it is written for, what it holds, what its claims
rest on, and what the platform requires it to carry.

Every post has exactly one type. Its authors choose it before publishing, and nothing changes it
afterwards.

The type sets what the post owes its reader: where it must give a source, what training it may
assume, and what must stand beyond the body of the text. It sets nothing else. A type does not
admit a post to the catalog, does not move it within any listing, and does not say who may write
it.

## Particulars

- A post that carries neither a source nor a method is of type `experience`.
- A source is given by its address where it has one, and in full where it has none.

## Implementation

| Type | Written for | What it holds | What it rests on | What it must carry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `scientific work` | People who work in the domain. It assumes their training | An investigation of the authors' own, in scholarly form | Its method, its data, and the work it cites | A source under every claim not the author's own, and method and data stated so that the work can be checked |
| `analysis` | Anyone | Known facts taken apart, and the authors' conclusions | The facts it analyses | A source under every fact analysed, and the reasoning laid open |
| `review` | Anyone | The state of a question, gathered from the work of others | The work it covers | A source for everything covered |
| `discovery` | Anyone | Something new the authors found: a find, an effect, a result | The find itself | What was found, and how it can be checked |
| `observation` | Anyone | What the authors saw in the world, first-hand | The authors | Where, when and under what conditions it was seen |
| `popular science` | Anyone. It assumes no training | Knowledge told to a reader who has no training in the domain | The work it cites | A source under every claim that is not common knowledge, and every term explained where it first stands |
| `experience` | Anyone. It assumes no training | What the author lived through or did, and what it taught them | The author | A source where the post uses one, and the bounds of the case: its conditions, its dates, how it ended |
