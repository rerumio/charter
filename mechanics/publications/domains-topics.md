# Domains of knowledge

## TL;DR

A domain is the top division of the platform — an area of knowledge or of life that a post belongs
to. Every post stands in exactly one, and reputation, access and admission are each held within one
domain rather than across all of them. Topics and keywords stand under a domain and narrow it.

## Definition

A domain is the top division of the platform: an area of knowledge or of life. Every post stands in
exactly one.

The domain is where things are counted. An account's reputation is held within a domain, access is
granted within a domain, and admission is decided within a domain — never across all of them at once.

Topics stand under a domain and narrow it. Keywords stand in one shared list across all domains.
A post takes any number of either.

A domain is opened and shaped by hand. No domain appears because posts gathered there, and none
disappears while posts stand in it.

## Particulars

- A post's authors set its domain, its topics and its keywords when writing the post, and never
  change them after.
- Writing a post, its authors name its keywords: a name already on the list binds to it; a name
  not on the list joins the list at publication.
- A domain, a topic and a keyword are identified apart from their names; renaming changes the name
  and nothing else.
- A merge moves every post of the merged topic into the surviving one; a topic or keyword is
  retired only empty.
- A holder of `taxonomy` creates topics; renames, merges and retires topics and keywords; and
  moves a single post between domains, topics and keywords. Nobody creates a keyword by hand —
  publication does.
- A holder of `domain` creates and shapes the domains themselves.
- Votes and reputation stay in the domain they were earned in; moving a post moves no reputation.
