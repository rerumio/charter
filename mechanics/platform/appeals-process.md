# Appeals

## TL;DR

An appeal is a written approach to whoever runs the platform — against a decision, or about
anything at all. Every appeal is answered within the term the eighth guarantee fixes, appeal and
answer are public, and an upheld appeal undoes the decision it names. An appeal suspends nothing
while it waits.

## Definition

An appeal is a written approach from a person to the platform, held under the eighth guarantee.

An appeal is of two kinds. An appeal against a decision names the decision it contests. An appeal
to the platform raises anything else. A holder of `answer` answers both.

Every appeal receives an answer that addresses what was asked. A review ends one of two ways: the
decision stands, or the appeal is upheld and the decision is undone.

An appeal and its answer are public records. An appeal decides nothing while it waits: the
decision it contests stands until the answer.

## Particulars

- Who writes, the term, and what stays public when an author closes their appeal stand in the
  eighth guarantee.
- The term runs from the day the appeal is made.
- Every appeal stands in one open register.

## Implementation

The term: 30 days, fixed by the eighth guarantee.

| State | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Open | Made and not yet answered. |
| Answered | Holds its answer and the answer's date. |
| Overdue | Past the term without an answer. The mark stays, answer or no answer. |

An appeal against a decision ends in one of two outcomes:

| Outcome | What follows |
| --- | --- |
| The decision stands | The answer names the rule the decision rested on. |
| Upheld | The decision is undone; what it took away returns, as a public record. |
