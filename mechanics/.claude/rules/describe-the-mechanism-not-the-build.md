# Describe the mechanism, not the build — this is the foundation, not the system

Write "the catalog endpoint returns results paginated and sorted by weight" and the law is now
welded to one stack. Rewrite the stack and the law is false; keep the law and the rewrite is
blocked. What belongs here outlives three rebuilds: what a thing is, what it is counted from,
what states it moves between, and who may act on it.

- **Name the rule, never the thing that runs it.** No endpoints, components, tables, queries,
  caches, frameworks, screens or libraries. "An edited post carries a mark that it was
  edited" — not "an edited flag is a column on the posts table".
- **Name figures, never fix them.** A clause says a figure exists and what it governs; the
  value lives in a published table outside the charter. A threshold written into a clause goes
  stale and drags the clause down with it.
- **Give every grant its inverse.** A clause that hands something out and never says how it is
  taken back is unfinished. Grant and revoke, admit and revoke admission, verify and revoke.
- **Follow a change through the whole system.** Rank, reputation, medals, vote weight, access
  and admission are one machine in several files. Changing what feeds vote weight changes what
  enters the catalog. Read the others before editing one.
- **Use one word per term, exactly.** A synonym that reads better breaks the ability to quote
  the law against itself.
- **The exception.** State an implementation constraint where a guarantee demands one —
  immutability of a post after its editing window, a permanent address, an append-only record. That is
  foundation, and it belongs here.
